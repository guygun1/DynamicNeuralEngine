solar eclipse ~ what is a solar eclipse? ~ explain a solar eclipse | A solar eclipse happens when the Moon passes between Earth and the Sun and blocks some or all of the Sun from an observer's view. It can happen only around a new moon, but not at every new moon, because the Moon's orbit is tilted relative to Earth's orbit around the Sun.\n\nWhether an observer sees a total, partial, or annular eclipse depends on the alignment and apparent sizes of the Sun and Moon. The event is a predictable consequence of orbital motion, not a change in the Sun's own energy production.
what are the stages of a solar eclipse? ~ solar eclipse stages | A solar eclipse begins when the Moon appears to move across the Sun's disk. During a partial phase, an increasing portion of the Sun is hidden. If the observer lies in the path of totality, a total phase follows in which the bright solar disk is completely covered for a short time.\n\nAfterward, the Moon moves away from the alignment and the visible portion of the Sun grows again. Observers in different places can see different phases or miss the event entirely. Ordinary sunglasses are not suitable protection for looking at the Sun.
why is a solar eclipse not visible everywhere? ~ solar eclipse visibility | A solar eclipse is visible only from places reached by the Moon's shadow or partial shadow. The region for a total eclipse is much narrower than the entire sunlit half of Earth, so totality is seen along a limited path.\n\nEarth rotates while the Moon moves in its orbit, causing the shadow to sweep across the surface. People outside the central path may see a partial eclipse, and people farther away may see none. Clouds and local weather can also prevent a clear view even when the geometry is favorable.
what is an annular solar eclipse? ~ annular solar eclipse | An annular solar eclipse occurs when the Moon passes in front of the Sun but appears too small to cover the entire bright disk. A ring of the Sun remains visible around the Moon. This is possible because the Moon's distance from Earth changes along its elliptical orbit.\n\nAn annular eclipse is different from a total eclipse, in which the Moon covers the full bright disk for an observer on the central path. The visible ring remains bright, so suitable solar-viewing protection is needed throughout the annular event.
what is a lunar eclipse? ~ lunar eclipse | A lunar eclipse occurs when Earth passes between the Sun and the Moon and Earth's shadow falls on the Moon. It happens around a full moon, but not at every full moon because the orbital planes are tilted relative to one another.\n\nDuring a total lunar eclipse, some sunlight reaches the Moon after passing through Earth's atmosphere, often giving it a reddish appearance. Lunar eclipses differ from solar eclipses in their geometry and in the region from which they can be observed.
food ~ what is food? ~ explain food ~ tell me about food | Food supplies the energy and materials that living organisms need to maintain themselves, grow, and carry out daily activities. For humans, eating is also connected to pleasure, family life, cultural traditions, and social relationships. No single food provides every nutrient in the right amount for every person.\n\nA useful way to think about food is to consider nutrition, variety, access, preparation, and sustainability together. Personal needs differ with factors such as age, activity, health, and available resources. A balanced discussion of food should therefore include both biology and the circumstances in which people live.
how does food provide energy? ~ food energy ~ explain food energy | The body can release chemical energy from nutrients in food through metabolic processes. Carbohydrates and fats are major energy sources, and proteins can also be used for energy while serving many structural and functional roles. Cells transfer some of the released energy into forms they can use for work.\n\nEnergy needs depend on a person's body, activity, and circumstances. Food labels estimate energy content, but a healthy diet involves more than counting energy alone. Vitamins, minerals, fiber, water, and the overall pattern of eating also matter.
what nutrients are found in food? ~ food nutrients ~ explain food nutrients | Important components of human diets include carbohydrates, proteins, fats, vitamins, minerals, and water. Fiber is also valuable, even though human digestive enzymes do not break it down in the same way as many other carbohydrates. Different foods provide different mixtures of these components.\n\nProteins supply amino acids used in tissues and many cellular processes. Fats support membranes and other functions as well as providing energy. Vitamins and minerals are needed in smaller quantities but remain essential. Variety helps people obtain a suitable range of nutrients over time.

Recipes and preparation methods can carry knowledge across generations, while ingredients often reflect geography, trade, migration, and local history.\n\nCultures are diverse and continually changing, so a cuisine should not be treated as a fixed set of dishes shared identically by everyone in a group. how is food produced? ~ food production ~ explain food production | Food production includes growing crops, raising animals, fishing, gathering, processing, and preparing ingredients for consumption. These activities depend on land, water, labor, energy, knowledge, infrastructure, and functioning ecosystems. Food then often passes through storage, transport, markets, and kitchens before it is eaten.\n\nDifferent production systems have different benefits, risks, and environmental effects. what is food sustainability? ~ sustainable food ~ explain food sustainability | Food sustainability concerns whether a food system can meet present needs while maintaining the environmental and social conditions needed for the future. Relevant issues include soil health, water use, greenhouse gas emissions, biodiversity, working conditions, and access to nutritious meals.\n\nThere is no single choice that solves every issue in every place. Reducing avoidable waste, improving production practices, and making nutritious food accessible can all contribute. Evaluating a proposal requires considering its full effects rather than judging it only by one label or one stage of production.
why is food waste a problem? ~ food waste ~ explain food waste | Food waste matters because producing food uses resources such as land, water, labor, and energy. When edible food is discarded unnecessarily, some of those resources are used without providing their intended benefit. Disposal can also create environmental costs.\n\nWaste can occur during harvesting, storage, transport, retail, preparation, and consumption. Better storage, realistic planning, suitable portion sizes, and improved systems for using surplus food can help. Prevention should also respect food safety and the practical circumstances of households and businesses.
what is food safety? ~ food safety ~ explain food safety | Food safety involves reducing the risk that food causes illness. Important practices include keeping hands and equipment clean, separating raw ingredients from ready-to-eat food, cooking appropriately, and storing perishable foods under suitable conditions. Requirements depend on the particular food and setting.\n\nMicroorganisms can multiply when conditions allow, and harmful contamination is not always visible or detectable by smell. Reliable local guidance is useful for specific cooking temperatures and storage times. General principles help, but they do not replace detailed instructions for a particular situation.
what is food security? ~ food security ~ explain food security | Food security exists when people have reliable physical and economic access to enough safe and nutritious food for an active, healthy life. Producing enough food in total does not automatically ensure that every household can obtain it. Income, infrastructure, conflict, climate, and policy can all affect access.\n\nImproving food security may involve supporting livelihoods, protecting supply chains, strengthening social support, reducing waste, and making nutritious choices affordable. It requires attention to both immediate hunger and the longer-term conditions that make a food system dependable.
