# General human knowledge, everyday reasoning, geography, and civic concepts.
what is geography? ~ explain geography ~ tell me about geography | Geography studies places, environments, and the relationships between people and the physical world. Physical geography examines features such as landforms, water, and climate, while human geography studies populations, settlements, economies, and cultures.\n\nMaps are useful tools, but geography is more than memorizing locations. It also asks why places differ, how people use resources, and how movement and environmental change connect distant regions. Political borders and place names can change, so context matters.
what are continents? ~ explain continents ~ how many continents are there? | Continents are very large land regions used to organize descriptions of Earth. A common seven-continent model lists Africa, Antarctica, Asia, Europe, North America, Australia, and South America. Other educational traditions use six-continent or other models.\n\nThe differences come from how connected land areas are grouped and from historical conventions. Continents are not the same thing as tectonic plates, and their boundaries are not all defined by one universal physical rule.
what are the oceans? ~ explain the world's oceans ~ how many oceans are there? | Earth's oceans form one connected body of salt water. A common modern classification names five major oceans: the Pacific, Atlantic, Indian, Southern, and Arctic. The boundaries between them are conventional divisions within this connected system.\n\nOceans store and move heat, support a great range of life, influence climate, and connect societies through transport and trade. They also face pressures from warming, pollution, overfishing, and habitat damage.
what is democracy? ~ explain democracy ~ tell me about democracy | Democracy is a form of government in which people have a meaningful role in choosing leaders and influencing public decisions. Modern democracies often use elected representatives, regular elections, and institutions intended to make officials accountable.\n\nElections alone are not the whole system. Civil liberties, political participation, independent institutions, and protections for minorities also matter. Democratic arrangements differ between countries, and their quality depends on how institutions operate in practice as well as what written rules say.
what is a constitution? ~ explain constitutions ~ why do countries have constitutions? | A constitution sets out fundamental principles and institutions of a political system. It may define the powers of government, relationships among branches or levels of authority, and rights that public officials must respect. Some systems use one main written document, while others rely on several sources.\n\nA constitution can limit power, but a written rule is effective only when institutions and people uphold it. Interpretation, enforcement, and lawful amendment are therefore important parts of constitutional government.
what is the rule of law? ~ explain the rule of law ~ why does the rule of law matter? | The rule of law is the principle that public power should be exercised under laws rather than arbitrary personal decisions. Laws should be publicly accessible, applied fairly, and supported by institutions that can review official conduct.\n\nIt also involves meaningful access to justice and limits on abuses of authority. The idea does not mean that every law is automatically just; people can challenge unjust laws while also valuing fair procedures and legal accountability.
what are human rights? ~ explain human rights ~ why do human rights matter? | Human rights are rights and freedoms understood to belong to people because they are human, rather than because a ruler chooses to grant them as a favor. They include protections for personal security, equality, expression, education, and other aspects of a dignified life.\n\nDifferent legal systems protect rights through constitutions, treaties, legislation, and courts. Rights can raise difficult questions about interpretation and competing interests, but the central idea is that every person's dignity matters.
what is an economy? ~ explain the economy ~ tell me about economics | An economy is the system through which people produce, distribute, exchange, and consume goods and services. It includes households, businesses, governments, workers, institutions, and both paid and unpaid activity.\n\nEconomics studies how people and societies make choices when resources are limited. Prices and markets are important in many economies, but laws, public services, social relationships, and environmental conditions also affect outcomes. No single number captures every aspect of economic well-being.
what is inflation? ~ explain inflation ~ why do prices rise? | Inflation is a sustained increase in the general level of prices, which means a unit of money buys less on average. It is usually measured using an index that tracks a selection of goods and services. Individual prices can change differently from the overall index.\n\nInflation can reflect several interacting influences, including demand, production costs, supply disruptions, expectations, and monetary conditions. Its effects vary between people, especially when wages, savings, debts, and essential expenses adjust at different rates.
what are supply and demand? ~ explain supply and demand ~ how do supply and demand affect prices? | Demand describes how much buyers are willing and able to purchase at different prices. Supply describes how much sellers are willing and able to offer. Their interaction helps explain how prices and quantities change in many markets.\n\nFor example, a sudden shortage can put upward pressure on a product's price. Actual outcomes also depend on competition, market power, rules, expectations, and how quickly production or consumption can adjust. The simple model is useful without explaining every feature of a real economy.
what is a budget? ~ explain budgeting ~ how can someone make a budget? | A budget is a plan for income, spending, and saving over a period of time. Making one usually starts with estimating reliable income, listing essential costs, and identifying irregular expenses as well as everyday purchases.\n\nComparing the plan with what actually happens helps reveal where adjustments are needed. A useful budget should reflect a person's real circumstances rather than assume everyone has the same income or choices. Unexpected costs are one reason people often try to keep some money in reserve when possible.
what is compound interest? ~ explain compound interest ~ how does compound interest work? | Compound interest means interest is calculated on both the original amount and previously accumulated interest. Over time, repeated compounding can make a balance grow faster than simple interest, which is calculated only on the original principal.\n\nThe result depends on the rate, time, compounding schedule, and any deposits, withdrawals, or fees. Compounding can help savings grow, but it can also increase debt. Comparing financial products therefore requires attention to their full terms, not just an advertised percentage.
what is education? ~ explain education ~ why is education important? | Education is the process of developing knowledge, skills, understanding, and habits of inquiry. It happens through schools and universities, but also through families, workplaces, communities, practice, and independent study.\n\nGood education helps people ask useful questions, evaluate evidence, communicate, and apply what they learn. Learning facts is valuable, but so is understanding their limits and connections. People can continue learning throughout life, and different learners may benefit from different kinds of support.
what is critical thinking? ~ explain critical thinking ~ how can i evaluate a claim? | Critical thinking means examining a claim carefully rather than accepting or rejecting it only because it feels familiar. Useful questions include what evidence supports it, how reliable the source is, what assumptions it uses, and whether other explanations fit the observations.\n\nIt also involves checking your own reasoning and being willing to change your view. Being critical is not the same as being cynical: some claims are strongly supported, while others deserve uncertainty or rejection.
what is the difference between correlation and causation? ~ explain correlation and causation ~ does correlation prove causation? | Correlation means two measurements tend to vary together. Causation means a change in one factor helps produce a change in another. A correlation alone does not establish a causal relationship because the pattern might result from another influence, reverse causation, or chance.\n\nResearchers use study design, experiments, timing, comparisons, and background knowledge to investigate causes. A controlled experiment can be especially helpful, but some questions require other forms of evidence because experiments would be impractical or unethical.
what is probability? ~ explain probability ~ tell me about probability | Probability is a mathematical way to describe uncertainty about events. A probability of zero means an event is impossible within the model, while a probability of one means it is certain within that model. Values between them describe different degrees of likelihood.\n\nProbabilities depend on assumptions and available information. A prediction can be reasonable and still fail on one occasion. Evaluating predictions fairly often requires looking at many cases and checking whether stated probabilities match observed frequencies over time.
what is an average? ~ explain mean median and mode ~ what is the difference between mean and median? | Several statistics can describe a typical value. The arithmetic mean is the sum of the values divided by how many values there are. The median is the middle value after sorting, while the mode is a most frequent value.\n\nThese measures can differ substantially when a dataset is uneven or contains extreme values. Choosing an appropriate summary depends on the question. Looking at the spread and shape of the data is often as important as reporting a single average.
what is culture? ~ explain culture ~ tell me about culture | Culture includes shared practices, ideas, languages, arts, values, knowledge, and ways of organizing social life. People learn and reshape culture through interaction, and individuals can participate in several overlapping cultural communities.\n\nCultures change over time and are not uniform collections of rules followed identically by every member. Understanding a community requires attention to its internal diversity, history, and circumstances rather than relying on stereotypes about what all its members supposedly think or do.
what is language? ~ explain language ~ why do humans use language? | Language is a system of communication that uses structured signs, such as speech, writing, or gestures, to convey meaning. Human languages allow people to describe events, ask questions, share knowledge, express feelings, and coordinate activities.\n\nMeaning depends on vocabulary, grammar, context, and shared expectations. Knowing isolated words does not guarantee understanding a sentence. Languages also change as communities use them, and signed languages are full languages rather than simple substitutes for spoken words.
what is the internet? ~ explain the internet ~ how does the internet work? | The internet is a global system of interconnected computer networks. Devices exchange data using agreed communication protocols, and information is often divided into packets that can travel across several networks before reaching a destination.\n\nThe web is one service that uses the internet, alongside email and many other applications. Infrastructure, software, addressing systems, and network agreements all contribute to communication. Using the internet does not by itself guarantee that information found there is accurate or that a connection is private.
what is an algorithm? ~ explain algorithms ~ tell me about algorithms | An algorithm is a well-defined procedure for solving a problem or carrying out a task. It describes steps that can be followed in a specified way, such as sorting a list or finding a route through a network.\n\nAlgorithms can be evaluated for correctness, efficiency, and how well they fit their intended use. A procedure can be precise and still produce a poor result if its assumptions, input data, or objective are unsuitable. Algorithms are ideas that can be implemented in different programs.
what is renewable energy? ~ explain renewable energy ~ tell me about renewable energy | Renewable energy comes from sources that are replenished on human timescales, including sunlight, wind, flowing water, and some forms of geothermal and biomass energy. Different sources require different technologies and have different environmental effects.\n\nRenewable does not mean impact-free. Land use, materials, ecosystems, reliability, storage, and transmission all matter when designing an energy system. Comparing options fairly involves considering their full life cycles and the needs of the communities using them.
what is public health? ~ explain public health ~ why is public health important? | Public health focuses on improving health across populations. It includes clean water, sanitation, vaccination programs, disease surveillance, safer environments, and efforts to understand how social conditions affect illness and well-being.\n\nClinical medicine often focuses on helping an individual patient, while public health also asks how to prevent harm across a community. The two approaches support each other. Effective public health depends on evidence, trustworthy communication, access to services, and attention to differences in people's circumstances.
what is empathy? ~ explain empathy ~ why does empathy matter? | Empathy involves trying to understand another person's perspective or emotional experience. It can help people listen more carefully, cooperate, and recognize that the same event may affect different people in different ways.\n\nUnderstanding someone's feelings does not require agreeing with every conclusion or action. It works best alongside respect, clear boundaries, and careful attention to what the person actually says. Assuming you already know exactly how someone feels can get in the way of genuine understanding.
