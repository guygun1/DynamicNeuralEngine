# Original explanations, with paragraph breaks encoded as \n.
what is science? ~ explain science ~ tell me about science | Science is a systematic way of investigating the natural world through observation, measurement, models, and tests. Scientists develop explanations that can be checked against evidence and revised when better evidence becomes available.\n\nA useful scientific claim should be clear enough to investigate. Confidence grows when independent observations and experiments support the same explanation. Scientific knowledge can be reliable while remaining open to correction; acknowledging uncertainty is part of the method rather than a failure of it.
what is the scientific method? ~ explain the scientific method ~ how do scientists test ideas? | The scientific method is a collection of practices for asking questions, forming testable explanations, making predictions, and comparing those predictions with evidence. It is not one rigid sequence that every investigation follows exactly.\n\nFor example, someone investigating plant growth might vary the amount of light while keeping other conditions similar. Measurements can then help distinguish the proposed explanation from alternatives. Repetition, transparent methods, and criticism from other researchers help identify mistakes and improve the explanation.
what is an atom? ~ explain atoms ~ tell me about atoms | An atom is the smallest unit of a chemical element that retains that element's chemical identity. It contains a small nucleus of protons and usually neutrons, surrounded by electrons described by quantum mechanics. The number of protons identifies the element.\n\nAtoms can gain, lose, or share electrons during chemical reactions. They can also join to form molecules and larger structures. Ordinary chemical reactions rearrange atoms and electrons rather than changing one element into another; nuclear reactions can change nuclei.
what is a molecule? ~ explain molecules ~ tell me about molecules | A molecule consists of atoms bonded together as a discrete unit. A water molecule, for example, contains two hydrogen atoms and one oxygen atom. The arrangement of atoms and the bonds between them help determine a substance's properties.\n\nNot every substance is made of separate molecules: metals and many salts form extended structures. Distinguishing atoms, molecules, and larger structures helps explain why materials can behave differently even when they contain some of the same elements.
what is energy? ~ explain energy ~ tell me about energy | Energy is a physical quantity associated with a system's capacity to produce changes, such as motion, heating, or electrical effects. Useful forms include kinetic energy, gravitational potential energy, chemical energy, and energy carried by light.\n\nEnergy can be transferred between objects and transformed between forms. In an isolated system, total energy is conserved. A device can become less useful as energy spreads into its surroundings as heat, even though the total amount of energy has not disappeared.
what is gravity? ~ explain gravity ~ tell me about gravity ~ how does gravity work? | Gravity is the interaction that makes objects with mass attract one another. Newton's theory describes many everyday and astronomical situations very well: the attraction depends on the masses and their separation. Einstein's general relativity describes gravity in terms of curved spacetime.\n\nGravity helps keep planets in orbit and gives objects weight near Earth's surface. Mass and weight are different: mass describes an object's inertia, while weight depends on the gravitational environment. An astronaut does not lose all mass when becoming weightless in orbit.
what is electricity? ~ explain electricity ~ tell me about electricity | Electricity involves electric charges and their behavior. Electric current is the flow of charge, while voltage describes a difference in electric potential. In a simple circuit, a source such as a battery can drive current through a conducting path.\n\nElectrical energy can be converted into light, motion, sound, or heat. Conductors allow charges to move relatively easily, while insulators resist that movement. Real circuits also depend on resistance, component design, and how the parts are connected.
what is magnetism? ~ explain magnetism ~ tell me about magnetism | Magnetism is a physical phenomenon associated with moving electric charges and the magnetic properties of particles. Magnets produce magnetic fields, and their north and south poles interact with other magnets. Electric currents can also create magnetic fields.\n\nElectricity and magnetism are closely related parts of electromagnetism. Their interaction makes technologies such as electric motors, generators, and transformers possible. Not every metal is strongly attracted to an ordinary magnet; iron is a familiar example of a strongly magnetic material.
what is light? ~ explain light ~ tell me about light | Light is electromagnetic radiation. Visible light is the part of the electromagnetic spectrum that human eyes can detect; radio waves, infrared, ultraviolet, X-rays, and gamma rays belong to the same wider spectrum.\n\nLight can show both wave-like and particle-like behavior. In a vacuum, it travels at about three hundred million meters per second. Reflection, refraction, absorption, and scattering help explain mirrors, lenses, colors, and many features of the sky.
what is sound? ~ explain sound ~ tell me about sound | Sound is a mechanical disturbance that travels through a medium such as air, water, or a solid. A vibrating source causes neighboring particles to move, passing the disturbance along. Sound cannot travel through an ideal vacuum because there is no material to carry that vibration.\n\nFrequency is related to perceived pitch, while the intensity of a sound contributes to how loud it seems. The speed of sound depends on the medium and its conditions, including temperature.
what is photosynthesis? ~ explain photosynthesis ~ tell me about photosynthesis | Photosynthesis is the process by which plants, algae, and some bacteria use light energy to build energy-rich molecules. In oxygen-producing photosynthesis, carbon dioxide and water are used to make sugars, and oxygen is released. Chlorophyll helps absorb light.\n\nThe sugars can support growth, storage, and other cellular activities. Photosynthesis supplies the starting energy for many food webs and has profoundly changed Earth's atmosphere. Plants also carry out cellular respiration, so photosynthesis is not the only process they use to manage energy.
what is cellular respiration? ~ explain cellular respiration ~ tell me about cellular respiration | Cellular respiration is a set of processes cells use to transfer energy from nutrients into useful chemical forms, especially ATP. In aerobic respiration, oxygen helps cells extract energy from molecules such as glucose, producing carbon dioxide and water.\n\nPlants, animals, and many other organisms carry out cellular respiration. It is related to breathing but is not the same thing: breathing moves gases into and out of an organism, while respiration refers to chemical processes within cells.
what is a cell? ~ explain cells ~ tell me about cells | A cell is a basic unit of living organisms. Cells are surrounded by membranes that help control exchanges with their surroundings. They contain molecular machinery for using energy, maintaining internal conditions, and carrying genetic information.\n\nSome organisms consist of a single cell, while others contain many specialized cells. Bacterial cells differ from the cells of animals and plants. In multicellular organisms, different cell types work together to form tissues and organs.
what is dna? ~ explain dna ~ tell me about dna | DNA, or deoxyribonucleic acid, is a molecule that stores hereditary information in most living organisms. Its sequence of chemical bases helps encode instructions involved in making proteins and regulating cellular activities.\n\nCells copy DNA during reproduction, and changes in the sequence can introduce genetic variation. Genes influence many traits, but development and behavior can also depend strongly on environmental conditions and interactions among many genes. DNA is not a simple instruction list that determines every detail of a person's life.
what is evolution? ~ explain evolution ~ tell me about evolution | Biological evolution is change in inherited characteristics of populations across generations. Mutation and recombination introduce variation, while natural selection, genetic drift, and gene flow affect how that variation changes over time.\n\nNatural selection occurs when some inherited variations influence survival or reproduction in a particular environment. Evolution does not require conscious planning and does not aim toward a predetermined perfect organism. Individuals develop during their lifetimes; populations evolve over generations.
what is an ecosystem? ~ explain ecosystems ~ tell me about ecosystems | An ecosystem includes living organisms and the nonliving environment with which they interact. Plants, animals, microorganisms, water, soil, light, and climate can all be important parts. Energy flows through food webs, while materials such as carbon and nitrogen move through cycles.\n\nChanges to one part can affect others, but these relationships are often complex. Protecting biodiversity can support ecosystem functions, including pollination, soil formation, and water regulation. Different ecosystems also respond differently to disturbance.
what are vaccines? ~ explain vaccination ~ how do vaccines work? | Vaccines help train the immune system to recognize a pathogen or part of it before a person encounters the disease itself. Different vaccines use different technologies, but the aim is to build protective immune memory.\n\nVaccination can reduce the risk of severe disease and, for some infections, reduce transmission. Protection and side effects vary by vaccine and individual circumstances. Medical recommendations are based on evidence about benefits and risks and should be discussed with qualified health professionals when personal decisions are involved.
what is the difference between bacteria and viruses? ~ explain bacteria and viruses ~ compare bacteria and viruses | Bacteria are cellular organisms that can often reproduce independently under suitable conditions. Viruses are much smaller infectious agents that rely on host cells to make more copies of themselves. Both groups contain a great variety of forms.\n\nMany bacteria are harmless or beneficial, while some cause disease. Antibiotics act against susceptible bacteria, not against viruses. Treatments and prevention depend on the particular infection, so the distinction matters when choosing an appropriate medical response.
what is the water cycle? ~ explain the water cycle ~ tell me about the water cycle | The water cycle describes how water moves among the atmosphere, land, oceans, and living organisms. Evaporation and transpiration move water into the air, condensation forms droplets or ice crystals, and precipitation returns water to Earth's surface.\n\nWater can then run into rivers, soak into the ground, become stored as ice, or return to the ocean. Solar energy and gravity help drive these movements. The cycle is not a single fixed route: water can remain in different stores for very different lengths of time.
what is plate tectonics? ~ explain plate tectonics ~ why do continents move? | Plate tectonics explains how Earth's outer rigid shell is divided into moving plates. These plates interact at boundaries where they can move apart, collide, or slide past each other. Their motion is connected to processes within Earth's interior.\n\nPlate interactions help explain many earthquakes, volcanoes, mountain ranges, and ocean basins. Continents move because they are parts of plates, not because they float independently across the oceans. The motions are usually slow on human timescales but have large effects over millions of years.
what causes the seasons? ~ explain the seasons ~ why do we have seasons? | Earth's seasons are mainly caused by the tilt of its rotation axis relative to its orbit around the Sun. As Earth travels around the Sun, each hemisphere changes how directly it receives sunlight and how long its days are.\n\nWhen the Northern Hemisphere is tilted toward the Sun, it generally has summer while the Southern Hemisphere has winter. The seasons are not mainly caused by Earth being closer to or farther from the Sun. Local climate also depends on factors such as latitude, elevation, and nearby oceans.
what is climate change? ~ explain climate change ~ tell me about climate change | Climate change means long-term changes in climate patterns, including average temperatures and precipitation. The current rapid global warming is primarily driven by human emissions of greenhouse gases, especially from burning fossil fuels and changing land use.\n\nGreenhouse gases affect how energy moves through Earth's atmosphere. Consequences include rising sea levels, changing extremes, and pressures on ecosystems and societies. Reducing emissions limits future warming, while adaptation helps communities deal with changes that are already occurring or expected.
what is the sun? ~ explain the sun ~ tell me about the sun ~ how does the sun produce energy? | The Sun is a star near the center of our solar system. It is a hot sphere of plasma composed mainly of hydrogen and helium. Nuclear fusion in its core converts hydrogen into helium and releases the energy that eventually reaches us as sunlight.\n\nThe Sun's gravity helps hold the solar system together, and its energy drives much of Earth's weather and supports most life. It does not have a solid surface like Earth. Scientists study it to understand stars and the space environment around our planet.
what is the difference between weather and climate? ~ explain weather and climate ~ compare weather and climate | Weather describes short-term atmospheric conditions, such as today's temperature, wind, rain, or cloud cover. Climate describes the long-term patterns and statistical behavior of weather in a region or across the planet, often using observations over decades.\n\nA cold day does not by itself disprove a warming climate, just as one hot afternoon does not establish a long-term trend. Climate research looks at many observations over time and considers natural variability as well as the underlying causes of change.
