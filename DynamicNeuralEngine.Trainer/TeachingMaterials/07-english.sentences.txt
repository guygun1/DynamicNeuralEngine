# Original graded sentences. Raw sequences supplement, rather than replace, dialogue training.
I am here.
You are there.
He is reading.
She is writing.
We are learning.
They are listening.
It is a book.
This is a chair.
That is a table.
These are apples.
Those are trees.
I have a pen.
She has a pencil.
We have two cups.
The cat sits on the mat.
The dog runs in the park.
The bird rests in the tree.
The fish swims in the pond.
A small child opens the door.
A tall person closes the window.
The red cup is on the table.
The blue bag is under the chair.
The green tree is beside the house.
The white cloud moves across the sky.
The yellow flower grows near the path.
I walk slowly.
She speaks clearly.
He listens carefully.
They work together.
We learn by practicing.
I like apples and pears.
She likes books and music.
We can read or draw.
The day is warm but the water is cold.
I stay inside because it is raining.
If the soil is dry the plant needs water.
First wash the cup.
Then fill the cup with water.
Finally put the cup on the table.
Before dinner we wash our hands.
After dinner we clean the table.
Yesterday I walked to the shop.
Today I am reading at home.
Tomorrow I will visit a friend.
The door was open yesterday.
The door is closed now.
The door will be open tomorrow.
I do not have a bicycle.
She does not eat meat.
They did not miss the bus.
We cannot see through the wall.
The empty box contains nothing.
The full basket contains apples.
A question asks for information.
An answer responds to a question.
A polite request can include the word please.
People can disagree and still listen to each other.
A mistake can be corrected with a clear example.
A word can have more than one meaning.
The meaning of a sentence depends on its context.
